Create the same old smartphone identity multiplied into an asynchronous phase-2 formation, shown in exactly four clean 2x2 cells: top-left three phones begin ringing at different angles, top-right four phones ring out of phase, bottom-left three phones spread farther apart, bottom-right four phones recoil independently. Do not draw grid lines, labels, captions, UI borders, or explanatory text.

Each cell must contain exactly three or four physically separate old smartphones, each with the same thick black bezel, scuffed casing, small round home button, and screen-facing-viewer identity established by phase 1. Space every phone apart with clear green gaps. Vary their positions, rotations, screen-on timing, and sparse solid pixel vibration marks so the ringing is visibly asynchronous. They are a coordinated threat but never touch, overlap into a pile, connect by wires, merge into one body, or form a single large phone.

The first reference is the authoritative style board. The second reference is only a runtime pixel-scale, outline, and palette-family reference. The phase-1 output is the authority for the individual phone design.

Pixel art only: chunky logical pixels, crisp nearest-neighbor edges, hard one-logical-pixel dark outline, top-left lighting, restrained 10-16 color palette, no anti-aliasing, no painterly rendering, no soft bloom. Ring signals must be separate blocky marks local to each active phone.

Background must be a perfectly flat, uniform, exact #00ff00 in every unused pixel. No hand, no table, no floor, no charger, no cable, no contact shadow, no cast shadow, no vignette, no gradient, no texture, no glow spill, no green variation. Leave generous green gutters between cells and between every phone.

Avoid: connected phones, phone pile, overlapping stack, one giant phone, modern bezel-less phones, flip phones, landline handsets, visible brand logo, words, names, digits, chat bubbles, notification cards, photorealism, 3D render, smooth vector art, watermark.
